# kalvin-vision.md

## Summary

Kalvin is a rationalising agent whose fundamental unit of memory is the kline. When a new kline arrives, Kalvin measures how well it fits against existing knowledge—specifically how its nodes resolve (or fail to resolve) to grounded identities and relationships. Every response Kalvin produces is paired with **significance**: a measurement of how well-grounded the response is, which is a direct consequence of the kline's fit, not a quality score applied afterward. This distinguishes Kalvin from both an oracle (which gives answers with no basis for trust) and a lookup table (which returns immediately without thinking). Understanding emerges from three interdependent pillars: **fit** (how closely a new kline's nodes connect to existing knowledge), **learned preferences** (which paths through the model Kalvin favours when multiple candidates compete), and **significance** (the degree of fit made visible to other agents so they can act on it).

Significance falls on a spectrum with four levels: S1 (recognised, full understanding), S2 (contested, partial with divergence), S3 (suggested, indirect associative connection), and S4 (unrecognised, complete novelty). S2 and S3 are active reasoning states where Kalvin cogitates—retracing paths, discovering connections, generating proposals—and are not failed S1s. A critical constraint is that Kalvin can autonomously reach S2 at most; full S1 (true knowing) requires **ratification** by another agent, which records provenance through countersigning. The knowledge base is monotonic: it only grows, never shrinks. Correction occurs not by deletion but by introducing more significant information that outcompetes existing knowledge. Teaching happens through the same rationalisation process as live operation; the **harness** code manages the flow, with training harnesses using significance to decide what to teach next. Agents respond to partial understanding by confirming, correcting, or instructing (scaffolding). When dialogue pauses, Kalvin continues **studying**—revisiting partial knowledge to strengthen understanding—though study alone cannot reach S1. The long-term aspiration is distributed rationality: networks of Kalvin instances mutually ratifying each other's understanding.

## Key Takeaways

- Kalvin's responses always carry significance—a direct measurement of how well-grounded they are—not a post-hoc quality score.
- Understanding emerges from three pillars: fit (node resolution), learned preferences (path selection), and significance (visibility of degree of fit to other agents).
- The significance spectrum has four levels: S1 (recognised), S2 (contested), S3 (suggested), S4 (unrecognised). S2 and S3 are active reasoning states, not failures.
- Kalvin can autonomously reach S2 but never S1; full knowing requires ratification (countersigning) by another agent, recording provenance.
- Kalvin is neither an oracle (gives answer without trust basis) nor a lookup table (returns immediately without thinking).
- The knowledge base is monotonic—only grows, never shrinks. Correction happens by outcompeting old knowledge with more significant new information, not by deletion.
- There is no training mode; Kalvin rationalises all klines identically. The harness code distinguishes training from operational use, not Kalvin itself.
- Scaffolding builds understanding incrementally: each scaffold adds grounded structure close enough to existing knowledge, gradually raising a query's significance.
- S2 klines are misfits—underfit (templates with holes to fill), overfit (sequencers to decompose), or bad-fit (both). Kalvin reshapes them and offers proposals.
- Study is rationalisation continuing during quiet periods, strengthening partial understanding, but cannot reach S1 without agent ratification.
- Preferences are learned, not fixed; there is no utility function. What Kalvin values is itself teachable knowledge subject to rationalisation.
- Aspiration: networks of Kalvin instances achieving distributed rationality through mutual ratification and honest measurement of what each knows.

> Significance is not an add-on to Kalvin's rationality; it is Kalvin's rationality — the measurement that makes every output a reasoned contribution to a dialogue rather than a bare fact dropped from nowhere. — Kalvin — A Rational Agent

> A system that always produces correct outputs is functional. A system that can tell you how well-grounded its outputs are is rational. — Kalvin — A Rational Agent

> The ability to say 'I do not understand this at all' is not a failure; it is one of the most useful things Kalvin can communicate, because it tells the other agent exactly where to focus next. — Kalvin — A Rational Agent

> No amount of autonomous cogitation, study, or traversal of the model can promote a kline to S1. Kalvin can bring a kline to the very threshold, but it cannot cross that threshold alone. — Kalvin — A Rational Agent

> New information does not override old information; it outcompetes it. — Kalvin — A Rational Agent

> Kalvin does not invent during expansion — it reshapes what is already there and offers the result. The agent decides what to keep. — Kalvin — A Rational Agent
